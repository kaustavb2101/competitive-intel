Consolidated Operating Results and Financial Position for the Year Ended 31 December 2025
Executive Summary
Muangthai Capital Public Company Limited successfully maintained its operating performance and
loan growth through both existing branches and newly opened branches in 2025, amid continuously changing
domestic and global economic conditions throughout the year. The Company has demonstrated its ability to
adapt effectively while continuously strengthening its internal capabilities under the strategic vision of “To Be
a World-Class Thai Microfinance.”
In 2025, Muangthai Capital Public Company Limited and its subsidiaries (the “Company”) reported
a consolidated net profit of THB 6,723 million, representing a net profit margin of 21.87% and an increase
of 14.59% compared with the same period of the previous year.
During 2025, the Company opened 518 new branches, bringing the total number of operating
branches to 8,673 as at 31 December 2025.
As at 31 December 2025, the Company’s total loan portfolio amounted to THB 183,222 million,
increasing by THB 18,980 million or 11.56% year-on-year. The majority of the loan portfolio is an comprised
secured loans. Non-performing loans totaled THB 4,628 million, accounting for 2.53% of the consolidated
loan portfolio.
Industry Outlook and Key Impacting Factors
1
In 2025, the Bank of Thailand (BOT) continued to implement an accommodative monetary policy.
The Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) reduced the policy interest rate several times from 2.25% to 1.25%
by the end of the year in order to support economic recovery and alleviate interest burdens on households.
This easing cycle contributed to a downward trend in funding costs for non-bank financial institutions (NBFIs)
and facilitated access to debt capital markets.
Industry competition remained focused on maintaining asset quality and customer base, as well as
managing net interest margins (NIM) through the procurement of lower-cost funding sources rather than
aggressive competition on lending rates. At the same time, debt relief measures introduced by the BOT-such
as the “You Fight, We Help” (YFWH) Phase 2 program and non-performing loan resolution through asset
management company (AMC) mechanisms-together with regulatory forbearance in cases of natural disasters
under the Responsible Lending framework, helped slow the inflow of non-performing loans and supported
loan restructuring efforts. These measures had a positive impact on overall portfolio asset quality.
Nevertheless, operators in the industry continue to face the need to carefully manage asset quality
risks, provisioning levels, and regulatory compliance in line with increasingly stringent BOT standards, as well
as to maintain adequate liquidity amid volatility in domestic and international money and capital markets.

As of 31 December 2025, the Company’s total liabilities were THB 149,463 million, increasing by
